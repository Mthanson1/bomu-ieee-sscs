\section{Introduction}
\texttt{BoMu} is a single-stage differential amplifier built from a resistively-loaded
NMOS source-coupled pair, designed for the SKY130A PDK and open-source TinyTapeout flow. Two
matched NMOS devices ($M_1$, $M_2$) share a tail sink of $2I_D$, and each drain is
pulled to the $1.2V$ supply through a load resistor $R_D$. The amplifier output is taken
differentially across the drains, $v_{od}=v(\text{voutp})-v(\text{voutn})$. A
floating differential source $\pm v_{sd}/2$ on a common-mode $V_{SC}$ drives the
gates through $100\Omega$ series resistors.

The driving bandwidth constraint is that each output drives an I/O pad of
fixed $C_L=5pF$. The tail sink is realised on-chip as a simple current mirror
(Section~\ref{sec:tailbias}) with a $200mV$ compliance budget. The flow is fully
open-source (\texttt{xschem}, \texttt{ngspice}, \texttt{magic}/\texttt{klayout});
devices are sized by a $g_m/I_D$ methodology~\cite{Jespers_gmID} from measured
current-density and speed data rather than an assumed square-law constant, and the
small-signal results are standard~\cite{Razavi_AnalogCMOS}.

This report documents the adopted $W=80\,\mu m$ design and signs it off at the
\texttt{tt} corner (with the native device-mismatch Monte Carlo of
Section~\ref{sec:quality}). The design is placed explicitly on the $g_m/I_D$
area--power trade (Sections~\ref{sec:areapower}--\ref{sec:cost}), where it sits at the
optimum of a power-weighted cost. The common-centroid \texttt{magic} layout is then
drawn, closed LVS/DRC clean, and re-simulated against its extracted parasitics
(Section~\ref{sec:layout}), which confirms both targets survive the layout.

\subsection{Net identities}
With the sign convention $M_1$ gate $=$ \texttt{vinp} $\rightarrow$ drain $=$
\texttt{voutn}, and $M_2$ gate $=$ \texttt{vinn} $\rightarrow$ drain $=$ \texttt{voutp},
the differential output $v_{od}=v(\text{voutp})-v(\text{voutn})$ has positive
gain with respect to the differential input $v_{id}=\text{vinp}-\text{vinn}$. The two
half-circuits are identical by matching, so the analysis is done on a single common-source
half-circuit and the differential result follows directly.

\section{First-Order Hand Design}
\label{sec:hand}
The design proceeds as follows: the fixed pad capacitance and the bandwidth target set the
load resistor, the gain target then sets the required transconductance, and a chosen
inversion level ($g_m/I_D$) fixes the bias current.

\paragraph{Load resistor from bandwidth.} The dominant pole sits at the output node,
formed by the load resistance working into the fixed $C_L$. Ignoring the device output
resistance for the moment,
\begin{equation}
    R_D \;=\; \frac{1}{2\pi f_{-3dB} C_L}
           \;=\; \frac{1}{2\pi(150\mathrm{MHz})(5\mathrm{pF})}
           \;\approx\; 212\Omega .
    \label{eq:RD}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Transconductance from gain.} The differential (half-circuit) gain of a
resistively-loaded pair is
\begin{equation}
    A_{v0} \;=\; g_m(R_D \| r_o) \;\xrightarrow{\;r_o \gg R_D\;}\; g_m R_D .
    \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
With $A_{v0}=10\mathrm{dB}=3.162\mathrm{V/V}$ and Eq.~\eqref{eq:RD},
\begin{equation}
    g_m \;=\; \frac{A_{v0}}{R_D} \;=\; \frac{3.162}{212\Omega} \;\approx\; 14.9\mathrm{mS}.
    \label{eq:gm}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Bias current from $g_m/I_D$.} Choosing a moderate-inversion
$g_m/I_D = 15\mathrm{V^{-1}}$ (a balance of device area, $r_o$, and speed at this
minimum length) fixes the per-branch current and the tail current,
\begin{equation}
    I_D \;=\; \frac{g_m}{(g_m/I_D)} \;=\; \frac{14.9\,\mathrm{mS}}{15} \;\approx\; 0.99\mathrm{mA},
    \qquad I_{\text{tail}} = 2I_D \approx 2\mathrm{mA}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Swing and headroom.} A $400mV_{pp}$ differential swing corresponds to
a single-ended peak of $v_{od}/4 = 100mV$ about each drain's DC level
$V_D = V_{DD}-I_D R_D \approx 0.99\,V$. Two conditions must hold: the bias drop must be
large enough to source the swing, $I_D R_D > 100mV$ ($I_D R_D \approx 210mV$ is ample), and the
device must remain saturated at the bottom of the swing,
$V_{D,\min}-(V_S+V_{ov}) > 0$, where $V_S$ is the tail-node voltage held at or above the
$200\,mV$ compliance budget. Both are comfortably satisfied because $R_D$ is small and the
supply headroom is large.

\section{$g_m/I_D$ Sizing Flow}
\label{sec:flow}
Equations~\eqref{eq:RD}--\eqref{eq:gm} fix $R_D$ and the required $g_m$. The $g_m/I_D$
method then turns the chosen inversion level into a physical width. A unit device is
characterised over $V_{GS}$ at fixed $(V_{SB},V_{DS})$ to build a lookup grid returning,
for any $g_m/I_D$, the current density $J_D$ (=$I_D/W$), the intrinsic gain $g_m r_o$, and
the transit frequency $f_T$. The width then follows as
\begin{equation}
    W \;=\; \frac{I_D}{J_D(g_m/I_D,\,V_{SB},\,V_{DS})} .
    \label{eq:W}
\end{equation}
This is implemented in \texttt{sim/sizing.py (worksheet)}, which references the \texttt{extern/sky130A-gmid}
lookup grid (a git submodule). The script takes a \texttt{DESIGN} dict (targets $+$ the two free
knobs, $g_m/I_D$ and $V_S$), prints the worksheet of
Table~\ref{tab:sizing}, and with \texttt{--spice} generates a differential-pair deck,
runs \texttt{ngspice}, and reports predicted-versus-SPICE side by side
(Table~\ref{tab:verify}).

\section{The $r_o$-Limited Gain}
\label{sec:ro}
Equation~\eqref{eq:gain} assumed $r_o \gg R_D$, but at minimum length this does not hold.
The lookup returns $r_o \approx 941\Omega$ at $g_m/I_D = 15$, so the effective
load is
\begin{equation}
    R_L \;=\; R_D \| r_o \;=\; 212 \| 941 \;\approx\; 173\Omega,
\end{equation}
and the realised gain and bandwidth become
\begin{equation}
    A_{v0} = g_m R_L \approx 2.58\ \mathrm{V/V}\ (8.23\mathrm{dB}),
    \qquad
    f_{-3dB} = \frac{1}{2\pi R_L C_L} \approx 184\mathrm{MHz}.
\end{equation}
Because $R_D$ is pinned at $212\,\Omega$ by the fixed $C_L$ and the bandwidth target
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:RD}), and $r_o$ at this length is comparable to $R_D$, the gain $+$ BW targets are
competing: the finite $r_o$ pulls the gain down to $\sim 8.2dB$ while pushing the
bandwidth up past $150MHz$. A single resistively-loaded stage at
$L=0.15\mu m$ cannot simultaneously meet $10dB$ and $150MHz$ for the selected $R_D$ at
the baseline current.

Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff} shows this: sweeping the inversion level with $g_m$
held at the gain-target value ($14.9\,mS$), the realised gain plateaus near
$8.3dB$ and never reaches $10\,dB$. At $L=0.15\,\mu m$ the intrinsic $g_m r_o$ is
simply too small for $r_o$ to dominate $R_D$, regardless of bias. The resolution
(Section~\ref{sec:hit}) is a small increase in $g_m$ --- i.e.\ a little more current ---
which lifts the realised gain--bandwidth product enough to clear both targets.

\end{figure}

despite it. The drain DC operating point of $0.988V$ matches $V_{DD}-I_D R_D$ exactly.

hence $W$, and $I_D$, $g_m$, $f_T$, $C_{gg}$, $r_o$, $g_m r_o$) all land inside $2\%$:

Section~\ref{sec:ro} that the small $g_m$ increase of Section~\ref{sec:hit} then
recovers to reach the adopted $W=80\mu m$ design.

\section{Design-Space Sweeps and the Gain--Bandwidth Limit}
\label{sec:sweeps}
Section~\ref{sec:ro} showed that at the chosen $R_D=212\Omega$, the finite $r_o$
leaves the gain short and the bandwidth long. This section sweeps $R_D$ and $L$ to map the achievable
space.

\subsection{$R_D$: trading gain for bandwidth at constant $g_m/(2\pi C_L)$}
For a single output pole the gain--bandwidth product is
\begin{equation}
    A_{v0}\cdot f_{-3dB}
    = g_m(R_D\|r_o)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi(R_D\|r_o)C_L}
    = \frac{g_m}{2\pi C_L},
    \label{eq:gbw}
\end{equation}
With $g_m=14.9mS$ and $C_L=5pF$ this
ideal product is $474MHz$. Sweeping $R_D$ (Figure~\ref{fig:sweeprd}) therefore slides
the design along this fixed hyperbola. A larger $R_D$ buys gain at the cost of
bandwidth. This sweep is at constant power, since $R_D$ draws no bias current. It is
also, by itself, not a route to the gain target: once the two targets pin the product,
no $R_D$ clears both at the baseline $g_m$ (quantified in
Section~\ref{sec:rdbracket}).

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=12cm]{Figures/bomu_sweep_rd.png}
\caption{$R_D$ sweep (iso-power $2.38mW$): gain rises and bandwidth falls along the
constant $g_m/(2\pi C_L)$ product. Dotted lines mark the $10dB$ and $150MHz$ targets.}
\label{fig:sweeprd}
\end{figure}

Channel length was also swept in an attempt to recover additional gain. Longer $L$ raises $r_o$, so
$R_D\|r_o\to R_D$ and the gain climbs toward the ideal $g_m R_D$ at constant power
(the current is unchanged, $W\propto L$ to hold the inversion level). The recovery is
modest and saturating: $8.2dB$ at $L=0.15\mu m$ up to only $\approx9.1dB$ by
$L=0.6\mu m$, which was still short of the $10dB$ target confirming the $r_o$-limited ceiling of
Section~\ref{sec:ro}. Length trades area for a fraction of the missing gain but does
not, on its own, reach the target.

\subsection{The design space: bandwidth versus power at fixed gain and swing}
\label{sec:frontier}
The $R_D$ sweep moved along one iso-power hyperbola; the next question is how
much bandwidth is available once power is
allowed to vary, subject to the fixed $10dB$ gain and $400mVpp$ swing. For the single
output pole, eliminating the load between $A_{v0}=g_m(R_D\|r_o)$ and
$f_{-3dB}=1/(2\pi(R_D\|r_o)C_L)$ gives the load-independent relation
\begin{equation}
    f_{-3dB} \;=\; \frac{g_m}{2\pi A_{v0} C_L}
            \;=\; \frac{(g_m/I_D)\,I_D}{2\pi A_{v0} C_L},
    \label{eq:frontier}
\end{equation}
so at fixed $A_{v0}$ and the fixed $5pF$ pad the bandwidth is set \emph{only} by $g_m$.
Since $P=V_{DD}\,I_{tail}=2V_{DD}I_D$, bandwidth therefore scales \emph{linearly with
power}, and the slope of that line is the $g_m/I_D$ efficiency. Figure~\ref{fig:frontier}
maps this plane: each line is one inversion level, swept over current.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=12cm]{Figures/bomu_design_space.png}
\caption{Design-space frontier: achievable $f_{-3dB}$ versus power at fixed $10dB$
gain and $400mVpp$ swing, one line per $g_m/I_D$ (slope $\propto g_m/I_D$). Dashed
segments are infeasible; the gold star is the adopted $W=80\mu m$ design on the
$g_m/I_D=15$ ray ($2.74mW$, $10.1dB$, $168MHz$).}
\label{fig:frontier}
\end{figure}

Note first that feasibility is essentially current-independent: the
headroom and swing walls are conditions on $g_m/I_D$, not on power, so each inversion
level is feasible (or not) across the whole power axis. The nominal feasible
window is wide, $g_m/I_D\approx9$ to $41$: below $\approx9$ the drain headroom fails
(the overdrive is large and, with $g_mr_o$ low, the gain-setting $R_D$ grows large
enough to collapse the drain), and above $\approx41$ the $R_D$ drop falls below the
$100mV$ single-ended swing. Within that window the only way to raise
bandwidth at fixed power is to raise $g_m/I_D$: at the $2.38mW$ baseline, moving from
$g_m/I_D=15$ ($150MHz$) up toward the swing wall would, on paper, reach
$\sim380MHz$ at the same power.

That wide window is misleading, though. BoMu holds a moderate
$g_m/I_D=15$ ($V_{ov}\approx130mV$) rather than chasing the swing wall for robustness:
a higher $g_m/I_D$ means a smaller $V_{ov}$ and less $g_mr_o$ margin, so the stage
becomes progressively more sensitive to the device spread the sizing is supposed to
tolerate, and the moderate inversion keeps that headroom. The practical lever for more
bandwidth is therefore power (the slope of Eq.~\eqref{eq:frontier}), not a freer
inversion level --- and, symmetrically, the only way to cut power at these specs is to
cut $g_m$, which gives back bandwidth one-for-one.

\paragraph{Where the design sits, and the minimum it costs.}
Figure~\ref{fig:gainpower} recasts the same plane with gain on the vertical axis,
which makes the hard-target minimum explicit. With $150MHz$ pinning the load at
$R_L\le212\Omega$, the gain at the bandwidth edge is
$A_{v0}=g_mR_L=(g_m/I_D)\,I_D\cdot212\Omega$, so each inversion level is a ray rising
with power (slope $\propto g_m/I_D$) until it saturates at the $g_mr_o$ ceiling. The
power at which a ray crosses $10dB$ is the \emph{ideal} minimum to meet \emph{both}
targets at that $g_m/I_D$ --- ideal because it assumes the realised gain--bandwidth
product equals the single-pole $g_m/(2\pi C_L)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gbw}. On the
$g_m/I_D=15$ ray adopted here that ideal floor is $2.38mW$. It is not reachable in
practice: as Section~\ref{sec:rdbracket} shows, second-order gate/Miller poles pull the
realised product $\sim1.5\%$ short, so the baseline at this power misses both targets by
a few percent. The adopted $W=80\mu m$ design therefore sits above the ideal floor at
$2.74mW$ once that gain-bandwidth-wall and the parasitic margin
(Sections~\ref{sec:hit}--\ref{sec:parasitic}) are added. Spending more
power moves the design \emph{up} its ray (more gain/BW margin) and spending less moves
it down toward the infeasible region --- the adopted point is a deliberately small step
above the ideal floor.

\subsection{The area--power plane: where each $g_m/I_D$ ray sits}
\label{sec:areapower}
The bandwidth- and gain-versus-power views priced each inversion level in \emph{power};
the third axis that matters on a fixed TinyTapeout tile is \emph{area}. This subsection
adds it, so the inversion level can be chosen on the full area--power trade \emph{before}
the operating current on the ray is fixed (Sections~\ref{sec:rdbracket}--\ref{sec:parasitic}).

Figure~\ref{fig:areapower} projects the $10dB$/$150MHz$ \texttt{tt} criteria onto the
area--power plane, one ray per inversion level, in the same house style as the
bandwidth- and gain-versus-power frontiers of Section~\ref{sec:frontier}. At fixed
$g_m/I_D$ the pair gate area is linear in power,
\begin{equation}
    2WL \;=\; 2L\,\frac{I_D}{J_D} \;=\; \frac{L}{V_{DD}}\,\frac{P}{J_D(g_m/I_D)},
    \label{eq:areapower}
\end{equation}
so each $g_m/I_D$ is a ray from the origin whose slope $\propto1/J_D$ steepens with
inversion level (weak inversion spends area fast). The $10dB$/$150MHz$ spec needs a
minimum $g_m$, hence a minimum current, so each ray is spec-feasible only above an
onset power (solid above, dashed below); the locus of those onsets is the \texttt{tt}
frontier. Along it, strong inversion (low $g_m/I_D$) gives small area but high power,
weak inversion the reverse --- area and power trade against each other, bounded below
by the drain-headroom wall and above by the output-swing wall of
Section~\ref{sec:frontier}.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering

The eventual operating point (gold star --- the $W=80\mu m$ design fixed in

conservative moderate inversion, on the $g_m/I_D=15$ ray, a small step above its

in the next subsection with a scalar cost.

\subsection{The $g_m/I_D$ optimum: a power-weighted cost}
\label{sec:cost}
Each $g_m/I_D$ ray in Figure~\ref{fig:areapower} meets the spec at a different
(area, power) pair, so choosing the inversion level is choosing a point on the
area--power frontier. To collapse that two-dimensional trade to a single preference we
score each feasible ray with a scalar cost and ask which $g_m/I_D$ minimises it
(Figure~\ref{fig:costgmid}).

\paragraph{Equal weighting is nearly flat.} The scale-free equal-weight product
$P_{\min}\!\cdot\!A$ gives the shallow grey bowl of Figure~\ref{fig:costgmid},
minimised at $g_m/I_D\approx12$ but \emph{very} weakly defined (the band
$g_m/I_D\approx10$--$15$ is within $+10\%$). The flatness is structural: near the
square-law limit $J_D\propto V_{ov}^2$ and $g_m/I_D\propto1/V_{ov}$, so
$P\!\cdot\!A\propto1/\big((g_m/I_D)^2 J_D\big)$ is nearly constant --- power and area
trade almost reciprocally, and their product barely discriminates. The tilt that
remains comes from the $r_o$-gain wall inflating power below the bowl and $J_D$
collapsing (area diverging) above it.

\paragraph{Power-weighting selects $g_m/I_D=15$.} Equal weighting is the wrong model
here: it prices a $\mu m^2$ and a $mW$ the same, whereas on a fixed TinyTapeout tile
area is effectively free and power is the real budget. Valuing power roughly twice the
area --- the scale-free weighted product $C=P^{0.66}A^{0.33}$ --- pulls the optimum
\emph{up} in $g_m/I_D$ (toward lower power), from $12$ to $\approx15$ (black curve). The
same shift lands at $g_m/I_D=15.0$ under a normalised weighted \emph{sum}
$0.66\,\hat P+0.33\,\hat A$, so the result is not an artifact of the sum-versus-product
choice. BoMu's $g_m/I_D=15$ is therefore the optimum of this power-weighted cost, and
the adopted $W=80\mu m$ design sits on that ray.

\centering

Two qualifications are worth stating. The $0.66/0.33$ weights are a design \emph{choice}
(power-limited tile), not a derivation; a different valuation moves the optimum, and the
bowl is shallow enough that anything in $g_m/I_D\approx13$--$17$ is near-equivalent, so
the cost function corroborates $g_m/I_D=15$ under a reasonable power-first weighting
rather than uniquely forcing it. Independently, a moderate $g_m/I_D$ also carries
the larger $V_{ov}$ and $g_m r_o$ headroom that makes the stage tolerant of device
spread (Section~\ref{sec:frontier}), so the power-weighted cost optimum and the
robustness-preferred value coincide at $15$.

\subsection{Sizing the current on the ray: is $R_D\approx280\,\Omega$ enough?}
\label{sec:rdbracket}
With $g_m/I_D=15$ fixed as the cost-optimal ray, the remaining freedom is the operating
current on it. The baseline sizing (Section~\ref{sec:spice}: $W=68\mu m$,
$I_{tail}=1.99mA$) sits at the bottom of that ray; this subsection confirms it cannot
reach both targets by re-tuning $R_D$ alone, which motivates the small current increase
of Section~\ref{sec:hit}.
The two targets have product $3.162\times150MHz=474MHz$, which is exactly the ideal
GBW of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gbw}. The specification therefore sits right on the
single-stage limit, reachable only if the realised product equals the ideal. It does
not: second-order gate/Miller poles pull the SPICE gain--bandwidth product down to
$\approx467MHz$, about $1.5\%$ short. Table~\ref{tab:rdbracket} brackets $R_D$ in SPICE
(\texttt{tt}, $L=0.15$, $W=68$); note no value of $R_D$ has sufficient GBW product at the baseline $g_m$.

\subsection{Layout-parasitic headroom and the adopted operating point}
\label{sec:parasitic}
The $g_m\!\approx\!16mS$ point only marginally clears the bandwidth target, and layout
will add output-node capacitance (drain-junction $C_{db}$ plus pad interconnect) on top
of the fixed $5pF$. Since $f_{-3dB}=1/(2\pi R_L C_{tot})$ falls with total capacitance
while the DC gain $g_m R_L$ is \emph{unaffected} (a capacitance is an open circuit at DC
and carries no bias current), only the bandwidth spec is exposed to parasitics. The
remedy is a small current margin: widening the pair to $\mathbf{W=80\mu m}$
($I_{tail}=2.28mA$) on the $R_D\approx245\Omega$ load lifts the nominal bandwidth well
past target with the gain comfortably over $10dB$. At this point the measured response
(real \texttt{res\_high\_po} loads and mirror, \texttt{tt}) is
\begin{equation}
    A_{v0}=10.1\,\mathrm{dB}, \qquad f_{-3dB}=168\,\mathrm{MHz},
    \qquad P=2.74\,\mathrm{mW},
\end{equation}
so the bandwidth carries $\sim12\%$ of headroom over $150MHz$ --- enough to absorb the
few-percent capacitive pole shift a compact layout adds while the gain, which
parasitics do not touch, holds its $\sim0.1dB$ margin. This is the \textbf{adopted
design}; its sizing is summarised in Table~\ref{tab:finalsize}.

\subsection{Tail-current mirror and input common-mode}
\label{sec:tailbias}
The ideal tail sink is realised on-chip as a simple two-transistor
resistor-programmed NMOS mirror, kept fully internal. A cascode/Wilson mirror needs
$V_{GS}+V_{DS,sat}\gtrsim0.5V$ of compliance, far above the tail budget, so only a
simple mirror fits. A raised input common-mode $V_{SC}=1.052V$ lifts the tail node
to $\approx0.33V$, putting the mirror in clean saturation while the pair retains
abundant drain headroom. The $400mV_{pp}$ swing survives (lowest single-ended output
$0.82V$, still well above the saturation floor; Section~\ref{sec:swing}).

The mirror uses standard-$V_T$ \texttt{nfet\_01v8} devices at $L=0.5\mu m$: output
$M_3=W80\mu m/nf8$, reference $M_4=W10\mu m/nf1$, ratio $N=8$, so the reference
leg draws only $I_{tail}/8\approx0.285mA$ through $R_{ref}\approx745\Omega$ from
$V_{DD}$. An integrated AC simulation (\texttt{sim/tb\_ac.spice}) with the real
mirror replacing the ideal sink closes the operating point and leaves the
differential spec essentially untouched (Table~\ref{tab:mirror}): at
$R_{ref}=745\Omega$ the tail settles at $0.333V$, $I_{tail}=2.28mA$,
$A_{v0}=10.19dB$, $f_{-3dB}=167MHz$ (ideal $R_D$; the real \texttt{res\_high\_po}
load trims this to the $10.1dB/168MHz$ of Section~\ref{sec:parasitic}). The gain
degrades by only $\sim0.03dB$ from the ideal source, as the mirror's finite $r_{out}$
lands on the common-mode path, not the differential one. The programming resistor sets
$I_{tail}$, so $R_{ref}$ is the single knob for the gain/BW trim. The mirror is folded
into \texttt{ip/bomu.sch} ($M_3$/$M_4$, $R_{ref}$); the complete schematic --- pair,
dummy-flanked loads and tail mirror --- is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}.

\section{Large-Signal Swing and Distortion}
\label{sec:swing}
The $400mV_{pp}$ differential output swing (Table~\ref{tab:specs}) is a large-signal
requirement, so it is checked with a dedicated transient testbench
(\texttt{ip/tb\_bomu\_swing.sch}, Figure~\ref{fig:swingtb}, which instantiates the
\texttt{bomu.sym} symbol hierarchically). A differential $1MHz$ sine rides on the
$V_{SC}=1.052V$ input common mode
with per-source amplitude $V_a=32mV$, so $v_{id,pp}=4V_a=128mV_{pp}$
drives the amplifier to the swing target. At the \texttt{tt} corner ($1.2V$,
$27^\circ$C) the measured swing is $v_{od,pp}=400mV_{pp}$, on target, with each
single-ended output spanning $0.824$--$1.024V$ about a $\sim0.924V$ common mode ---
inside the rails with every device still saturated, so the stage is not clipping. The
same deck reports the AC gain/bandwidth and the averaged supply power
($A_{v0}=10.1dB$, $f_{-3dB}=168MHz$, $2.74mW$ pair / $3.10mW$ total including the
reference leg), so swing, gain, bandwidth and power all come from one run.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=12cm]{Figures/Xschem_Output_Swing_TB.png}
\caption{The transient swing testbench \texttt{ip/tb\_bomu\_swing.sch}.}
\label{fig:swingtb}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Third-harmonic dominance.} A Fourier decomposition of $v_{od}$ (fundamental
$1MHz$, ten harmonics) gives $\text{THD}=0.61\%$, set almost entirely by the third
harmonic (Table~\ref{tab:thd}). Two features are characteristic of a balanced
source-coupled pair. First, the even harmonics vanish (at the $\sim10^{-14}$ numerical
floor): even-order distortion is common-mode and cancels in
$v_{od}=v(\text{voutp})-v(\text{voutn})$, so their absence confirms the devices and
loads are symmetric. Second, the residual distortion is odd-order, led by the third. Both features follow
from the \emph{shape} of the balanced pair's differential transfer
$i_{od}(v_{id})$, independently of any particular device law: it is an \emph{odd}
function of the differential drive (swapping the inputs negates the output), which is
precisely what forces the even harmonics to cancel, and it is \emph{compressive}
(the pair steers a fixed tail current, so $i_{od}$ saturates at $\pm I_{tail}$). An
odd, compressive characteristic has a Taylor expansion in odd powers of $v_{id}$
alone, so the leading nonlinearity is the cubic term,%
\footnote{There is no closed-form expression for $i_{od}(v_{id})$ at BoMu's
operating point. The often-quoted $i_{od}\propto\tanh\!\big(v_{id}/2nV_T\big)$ is the
\emph{weak-inversion limit} only, where the devices follow the exponential law
$i_D\propto\exp(v_{GS}/nV_T)$ ($V_T=kT/q$ the thermal voltage, $n$ the subthreshold
slope factor, highest $g_m/I_D$); the opposite strong-inversion limit is the
square-law $\surd$-characteristic. BoMu runs at $g_m/I_D\approx15$ (moderate
inversion), between the two, where neither closed form holds --- but the odd-symmetry
and leading-cubic arguments above hold throughout.} which folds back as a third
harmonic. The fifth and higher harmonics fall off by orders of
magnitude, hence $\text{THD}\approx$ the third-harmonic term alone. Because that term
scales with the cube of the drive, distortion climbs quickly with swing; at the
$400mV_{pp}$ operating point it stays a clean $0.61\%$.

\section{Small-Signal Quality: Offset, CMRR, PSRR and Noise}
\label{sec:quality}
Beyond the headline gain/bandwidth/swing targets, the pair is characterized for
input offset, common-mode and supply rejection, and noise, at \texttt{tt}/$1.20V$/
$27^\circ$C. These figures are measured on the \emph{extracted, post-layout} netlist
(\texttt{verify/bomu\_rcx.spice}, full $R+C$ RCX of the signed-off $W{=}80\mu m$ layout)
via \texttt{sim/corners.py acq rcx} and \texttt{sim/corners.py mc\_mm --pex rcx}, so the
numbers below carry the real device sizing \emph{and} the layout parasitics. One
point about the models frames every mismatch-dependent number. Contrary to a common
belief about the open PDK, sky130A \emph{does} carry native statistical device
mismatch: the \texttt{.pm3} model cards apply per-device Gaussian spread to
$V_{th0}$, $t_{oxe}$, $v_{off}$ and $n_{factor}$ through
\texttt{mc\_mm\_switch}$\cdot$\texttt{AGAUSS}($\ldots$) terms, live in
\texttt{nfet\_01v8\_lvt} (the pair), \texttt{nfet\_01v8} (the mirror) and
\texttt{res\_high\_po} (the loads). Because the extracted \texttt{.subckt} wraps these
same sky130 device instances, the native mismatch still redraws per device inside the
parasitic netlist. The offset below is therefore drawn from this native mismatch with
\emph{no} assumed Pelgrom coefficient (\texttt{mc\_mm\_switch} flipped via
\texttt{alterparam} after the \texttt{.lib} loads, then re-seeded per draw; see
\texttt{corners.py mc\_mm --pex}).

\paragraph{Input offset.} A $40$-draw native-mismatch Monte Carlo on the extracted
$W{=}80\mu m$ pair ($12\mu m^2$ per device) gives $\sigma(V_{os})\approx2.8mV$
($3\sigma\approx8.3mV$, worst draw $7.4mV$), centred on zero (mean $+0.6mV$). Because
offset grows as $1/\sqrt{WL}$, this is set by the pair size the gain--bandwidth sizing
already demands; it is modest by construction and protected by the common-centroid
layout of Section~\ref{sec:layout} --- indeed the extracted interdigitated pair sits a
little below the $\sim3.6mV$ a single lumped $W{=}80\mu m$ device predicts. Offset is
treated as a separate, un-targeted spec here.

\paragraph{CMRR and PSRR.} Because the output is taken differentially between
matched halves, common-mode input and supply ripple couple almost entirely into the
output common mode and cancel differentially. The differential CMRR and
PSRR are matching-limited, not tail-limited, and would be infinite for a perfectly
symmetric netlist; over the native-mismatch draws on the extracted netlist their median
is $\text{CMRR}\approx67dB$ and $\text{PSRR}\approx53dB$ (both scale inversely with the
per-draw mismatch, so they are quoted as the median of the $24$-draw set). The
underlying common-mode paths set the ceiling and are finite: the tail-limited
common-mode gain is $A_{cc}=-17.6dB$, and because $R_D$ couples $V_{DD}$ straight onto
each drain --- now reinforced by the asymmetric $V_{DD}$-to-drain coupling capacitors
the extraction adds --- the supply-to-common gain rises to $A_{sc}=-7.8dB$. This is
the same balance that suppressed the even harmonics in Section~\ref{sec:swing}.

\paragraph{Noise.} The input-referred noise of the extracted netlist integrated over
$10Hz$--$1GHz$ is $\approx93\mu V_{rms}$ (device thermal/flicker dominated, so it is
taken at the nominal matched point rather than per mismatch draw). Against the
$\sim45mV_{rms}$ differential input drive that produces the $400mV_{pp}$ output swing
(the $v_{id,pp}=4V_a=128mV_{pp}$ at $V_a=32mV$ of Section~\ref{sec:swing}), this is an
input SNR of order $54dB$. The integration band runs well past the $\sim164MHz$ output
pole, so the figure is conservative: above the pole the falling gain inflates the
input-referred density, and most of the integral accumulates beyond the usable band.

\section{Physical Layout in \texttt{magic}}
\label{sec:layout}
With the $W=80\mu m$ design signed off against the \texttt{tt} corner, the
\texttt{bomu} cell is drawn in \texttt{magic} on SKY130A from the adopted schematic
(Table~\ref{tab:finalsize}), following the matching and isolation methodology below.

\paragraph{Pair core.} The overriding layout concern for a differential pair is
matching of $M_1$/$M_2$, so the core is a common-centroid interdigitation of the
$nf=8$ pair. The $W=80\mu m$/$nf=8$ pair lays out as $10\mu m$-per-finger unit
devices on isolated diffusion islands, assigned to the two halves in an alternating
centroid pattern so a linear process gradient across the block cancels differentially.

\paragraph{Edge dummies.} Matching is protected at the array edges by same-width
$nf=1$ dummy transistors on the left and right columns, which give the outermost live
fingers an identical poly/diffusion neighbourhood ($10\mu m$ finger width, matching the
pair). Their gates, sources and drains are strapped to \texttt{VSS}. The load resistors
are handled the same way: the two live loads $R_{D1}$/$R_{D2}$
(\texttt{res\_high\_po\_5p73}) are flanked by two dummy resistors of identical geometry
for etch/density symmetry, terminated to \texttt{VSS}.

\paragraph{Tail mirror and guard rings.} The mirror lays out as a single
interdigitated row of \texttt{nfet\_01v8} unit fingers ($W=10\mu m$, $L=0.5\mu m$) with
the diode-connected reference $M_4$ at the centre and the eight $M_3$ output fingers
around it (a $1$-D common-centroid, ratio $N=8$); the programming resistor $R_{ref}$
(\texttt{res\_high\_po\_2p85}) sits beside it. Being an all-NMOS block, every sub-block
is wrapped in a p$^+$ guard ring tied to \texttt{VSS} with no n-well, and the six
top-level ports (\texttt{vinp}, \texttt{vinn}, \texttt{voutp}, \texttt{voutn},
\texttt{VDD}, \texttt{VSS}) are brought out on upper metal past the outer ring. The
finished cell is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:layout}.

\section{Status and Next Steps}
\label{sec:status}
The sign-off cockpit for the adopted $W=80\mu m$ design --- the block intended for
tapeout --- is collected below as a single-glance review summary of the targets, the
\texttt{tt} performance (schematic and post-layout RCX), the small-signal quality and the
physical-verification status. Every number is carried elsewhere in the document; this is
the consolidated view. All performance figures are at the \texttt{tt} corner,
$V_{DD}=1.20V$, $27^\circ$C; the quality figures are on the extracted netlist
(\texttt{verify/bomu\_rcx.spice}). The offset/CMRR/PSRR figures come from sky130A
\emph{native local device-mismatch} Monte Carlo only --- not a global process-corner MC,
so they are a matching check, not a full statistical sign-off.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Performance (\texttt{tt}; schematic $\rightarrow$ post-layout RCX).}
    Differential DC gain $A_{v0}=10.1\rightarrow10.19$ dB (target $\ge10$ dB,
    \checkmark); bandwidth $f_{-3dB}=168\rightarrow164.1$ MHz (target $\ge150$ MHz,
    \checkmark); output swing $400$ mV$_{pp}$ at $0.61\%$ THD (target $400$ mV$_{pp}$,
    \checkmark); power $P=V_{DD}I_{tail}=2.74$ mW ($I_{tail}=2.28$ mA).
  \item \textbf{Operating point (\texttt{ip/bomu.sch}).} Input pair $M_1,M_2$:
    \texttt{nfet\_01v8\_lvt} $W{=}80\mu m$/$L{=}0.15$/$nf{=}8$; drain loads $R_D$:
    \texttt{res\_high\_po} $\approx243\,\Omega$; tail mirror $N{=}8$ at

The central result (Section~\ref{sec:sweeps}) is that the $10dB$ / $150MHz$
specification sits on the single-stage gain--bandwidth wall $g_m/(2\pi C_L)$, which the
baseline sizing clears by only $\sim1.5\%$. A small $g_m$ increase and a parasitic
margin give the \textbf{adopted $W=80\mu m$ design}
($R_D\approx245\Omega$, $I_{tail}=2.28mA$, $2.74mW$), which meets both targets at the
\texttt{tt} corner --- $A_{v0}=10.1dB$, $f_{-3dB}=168MHz$, $400mV_{pp}$ swing at
$0.61\%$ THD --- and survives the native \emph{local} device-mismatch MC
(Section~\ref{sec:quality}), which moves offset; the differential gain and bandwidth are
expected to be left essentially unchanged by mismatch, since the common-mode and mismatch
terms cancel in a balanced pair --- though the draws were scored for offset and
CMRR/PSRR, not for a gain/BW spread, so this is an expectation rather than a measured
$\sigma(A_{v0})/\sigma(f_{-3dB})$. This MC covers per-device mismatch only; it is
\emph{not} a global process-corner Monte Carlo, so it is not a full statistical sign-off
--- a global process-corner MC (with the full $V_{DD}$/temperature grid) remains a future
step. On the area--power plane (Sections~\ref{sec:areapower}--\ref{sec:cost})
it sits near the joint minimum of area \emph{and} power the \texttt{tt} criteria allow,
on the $g_m/I_D=15$ ray that is the optimum of a power-weighted cost.

The physical implementation (Section~\ref{sec:layout}) is now complete and
\textbf{signed off}: the $W=80\mu m$ common-centroid pair, the dummy-flanked load bank
and the interdigitated tail mirror are drawn with \texttt{VSS}-tied guard rings, the
block closes \texttt{netgen} LVS \emph{match uniquely} and passes both the \texttt{magic}
and \texttt{klayout} \texttt{sky130A\_mr} DRC decks with zero violations, and the
extracted-parasitic (RCX) \texttt{tt} AC re-simulation confirms the $\sim12\%$
bandwidth margin absorbs the parasitic pole shift with the gain intact
(Table~\ref{tab:postlayout}). The signed-off GDS and reports are archived under
\texttt{tapeout/} and \texttt{verify/}.

The offset/CMRR/PSRR/noise characterisation of Section~\ref{sec:quality} is now run
directly on the extracted (post-layout) netlist: the native device-mismatch Monte Carlo
and the CMRR/PSRR/noise measurements all exercise \texttt{verify/bomu\_rcx.spice}, so
they carry both the adopted $W{=}80\mu m$ sizing and the layout parasitics. The headline
differential gain/bandwidth are expected to be essentially untouched by mismatch
(balanced-pair cancellation; the draws were not scored for their spread);
the quality metrics land at $\sigma(V_{os})\approx2.8mV$, $\text{CMRR}\approx67dB$,
$\text{PSRR}\approx53dB$ and $\approx93\mu V_{rms}$ input-referred noise, with the
supply-rejection path the most parasitic-sensitive ($A_{sc}$ rising from the ideal
$R_D$-coupling estimate once the extracted $V_{DD}$-to-drain capacitors are included).

\paragraph{Further improvements.} Several directions would push the design past its
current limits. (i) The headline constraint is the single-stage gain--bandwidth wall
$g_m/(2\pi C_L)$ (Section~\ref{sec:sweeps}), and the analysis there is explicit that the
\emph{only} lever on that wall is more $g_m$ --- bought either by spending more power
(moving up a $g_m/I_D$ ray) or by raising the $g_m/I_D$ efficiency itself (a steeper ray,
bounded above by the swing/headroom walls of Section~\ref{sec:frontier}). At fixed gain
and swing the bandwidth scales linearly with power at a slope set by that $g_m/I_D$
efficiency (Eq.~\eqref{eq:frontier}), and no load re-tuning escapes the $474MHz$ ideal
product (Eq.~\eqref{eq:gbw}, Table~\ref{tab:rdbracket}). Cascoding to raise $r_o$ would only slide
the design along that hyperbola --- more gain for less bandwidth --- and still miss both
targets at the baseline $g_m$, so it is not a way around the wall. What a cascode
\emph{can} do, applied to the \emph{input pair} rather than the load, is reduce the Miller
multiplication of $C_{gd}$ that forms the second-order gate pole --- the pole
Section~\ref{sec:rdbracket} found pulls the realised GBW $\sim1.5\%$ below the $474MHz$
ideal. An input-pair cascode could recover part of that $1.5\%$, nudging the realised
product toward the single-pole ideal rather than beating it, at the cost of the extra
stacked $V_{DS,sat}$ the $1.2V$ rail and $400mV_{pp}$ swing make scarce. (ii)
\emph{Active-inductor peaking} at the output pole would extend $f_{-3dB}$ at fixed $R_D$
and current; a passive spiral of the required value is far too large for the TinyTapeout
tile, so only an active inductor (a gyrator-C load) is practical, buying bandwidth margin
at the price of its own headroom and noise. (iii) The supply-rejection path is the weakest
small-signal link ($A_{sc}=-7.8dB$, Section~\ref{sec:quality}, set by $V_{DD}$ coupling
through $R_D$ onto the drains); a \emph{replica-biased or regulated drain load} would
attenuate that path and tighten the present $\text{PSRR}\approx53dB$ (median of the
$24$-draw native-mismatch set). (iv) The simple tail mirror is compliance-limited
(Section~\ref{sec:tailbias}); a \emph{low-voltage cascode or constant-$g_m$ tail} would
raise the tail output resistance --- improving on the present $\text{CMRR}\approx67dB$
($24$-draw median) --- and cut the supply/temperature sensitivity of $I_{tail}$, if the
$200mV$ budget can be relaxed or a cascode fitted within it. (v) If offset ever becomes a
spec, \emph{chopping or auto-zeroing} (or simply more pair area) would push below the
present $\sigma(V_{os})\approx2.8mV$ ($40$-draw native-mismatch MC). None of these beats
the gain--bandwidth wall --- Section~\ref{sec:sweeps} shows only more $g_m$ per mW does
that; they refine the realised design toward its ideal limit (input cascode), extend
bandwidth (active inductor), or harden the rejection and offset the quality section
measures.
